What the numerical instrument actually solves

A complex frequency-domain wave operator couples neighboring sites: A(h,ω,γ)u=F, with a tridiagonal spatial stencil and local material-dependent coefficient. The source is node 3, the receiver node 44. The teacher is injected at the receiver with the complex output error as its amplitude. Damping changes both amplitude and phase. Every model solve uses the actual current material state.

Each patch stores θᵢ, its old ordinary field energy. A single teaching exposure gives rᵢ = |uᵢ + τᵢ|² − θᵢ. The local G8 edge relay exchanges neighbor differences to make a zero-net-material update; it does not access a global mean. The control named “exact gradient” uses the analytic wave sensitivity and is not an autonomous material rule.

Claude's G8 warning: A recalibration that changes θ but forbids bed motion has an external privilege. The active-rest branch allows a second material update during a teacher-free exposure, then a T-rate baseline update. It needs an externally scheduled teacher-off gate, and may cost extra wave solves and additional material displacement. Compare the two rather than treating the privileged result as a physical prediction.

Read the plots as falsification tools: a positive cosine at epoch one does not imply the next nineteen steps remain aligned. Tracking the teacher can absorb the desired deviation, while never updating θ can cause staleness. Here the material is an effective numerical parameter, not bed depth constrained by an Exner sediment law. The synthetic loss/damping sweep is illustrative and does not reproduce the 2D cavity's measured Q.
